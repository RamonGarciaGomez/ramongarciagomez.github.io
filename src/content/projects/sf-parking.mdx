San Francisco writes about 4,500 parking tickets on a normal weekday, and almost
none of them are surprises. Street-cleaning crews follow published routes. Meter
enforcement switches on at 9 am sharp. Residential permit officers chalk tires
and come back exactly two hours later.

[The map](/projects/sf-parking) turns 13 years of SFMTA citation data — about 12
million geocoded tickets — into the question you actually have: *how ticketed is
this block, at this hour, on this day?* Alongside it, a set of computed
vignettes from the last 30 days of citations: the most-ticketed block in the
city, the biggest single-hour enforcement burst, and the one car that collected
27 tickets in a month.

The technically interesting part is the geocoder: the city stopped publishing
coordinates for citations in 2020, so the pipeline learns locations from the
historical record itself — 854,000 distinct address strings mapped from 11.9M
pre-2020 tickets, backstopped by the city's address registry and intersection
inference. 98.7% of brand-new citations resolve deterministically, in
microseconds, with no external geocoding service.
